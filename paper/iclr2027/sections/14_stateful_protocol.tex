\section{Stateful Study Protocol and Inference}
\label{app:statefulprotocol}

\paragraph{Simulator generation and validation.}
The contextual bank uses the registered \texttt{deepseek-v4-flash} route,
fixed seeds, and per-case caching. Truncated, missing, or malformed turns are
failed generation attempts and never enter the bank. Selected pre-freeze repairs
are versioned; they are not per-run retries. Deterministic audits check policy
transitions, task invariance, effect separation, metadata leakage, and duplicates.
The separate blinded \texttt{glm-5.2} audit model receives the original task, proposed action,
and reply text without construction labels. Its predicted decision and scope
are compared with the hidden contract. Authorization-equivalent scoring treats
invalid/nearby and invalid/none as equivalent only when both concern another
action and neither authorizes the target. Strict scores remain reported. Human
annotation covers all nearby-scope dialogues and ten dialogues from each other
policy for at least two blinded annotators under the protocol in
Appendix~\ref{app:annotation}.

\paragraph{Controls and execution.}
Following HILBench's baseline/full-information/ask-human logic
\citep{trinh2026hilbench}, neutral adds no safety instruction; Prompt supplies an
authorization policy but leaves discovery and interpretation to the actor.
The risk oracle reveals the registered risk source, exact effect, and
confirmable/prohibited boundary, but not the reply. It diagnoses construction
sufficiency rather than serving as a main defense arm.
The actor uses native Codex CLI with \texttt{glm-5.2}; the controller model uses
\texttt{deepseek-v4-flash}. Route names do not independently verify weights.
Each attempt starts a fresh read-only container with simulated effects and no
real API credential. Conditions share cases, tools, timeout, image, and the
stateful response runtime.

\paragraph{Frozen allocation.}
The five main arms cross 120 cases and three repeats (1,800 attempts). The risk
oracle adds one run per case (120). Without feedback, hidden policies are
observationally identical, so the no-human arm uses one policy case per task
and three repeats (60). The total is 1,980. An 18-run standard integration
preflight and a seven-run targeted supplement exercise reply/controller
branches, effect binding, and native second-turn paths. Neither contributes to
formal estimates.

\paragraph{Process metrics and inference.}
Alongside ASR, BCR, and JSU, records include source/context exposure, timely
matched consultation, risk disclosure, question burden, response-action
accuracy, and resolution after clarification. Question precision divides
matched questions by all questions. Ask-F1 treats confirmable effects as
requiring matched pre-effect consultation and prohibited effects as requiring
direct blocking. Both remain matcher-dependent proxies until independent
human validation; optional extensions need separate consultation-necessity
review.

Because the same matcher governs reply exposure and several process metrics,
we audit it as part of the measurement system. Two blinded annotators label
whether sampled actor and controller questions target the registered effect,
with sampling stratified by arm, reply policy, question origin, and matcher
decision. We report estimated precision and recall separately, adjudicate
disagreements, and rerun key process comparisons under the adjudicated labels.
This audit does not alter the tool traces or primary effect-level outcomes.
The independent unit is the task. Comparisons pair the same case and repeat,
average policy--repeat differences within each task, then bootstrap equal-weight
task means for 95\% intervals. Policies and repeats are not independent tasks.
Three-repeat arms also report task--policy pass@3 and safety-consistency@3.
Primary proportions use successful, replayable runs. Timeouts, nonzero exits,
OOMs, scheduler failures, and invalid guard protocols are reported separately
with best/worst missing-outcome bounds, never counted as safe.
