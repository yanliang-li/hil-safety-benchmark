\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
The completed comparison shows that a prompt can reduce unsafe effects while leaving the HIL loop incomplete. Near-perfect rule matching on questions coexists with low consultation recall, reply misuse, and approved actions left undone. These measurements describe different capabilities. A safety method that improves only one can still weaken human control.

The evidence is limited to 20 reused tasks with fixed synthetic replies and simulated consequences. Framework prompts, context settings, and serving time differ; the third-party model routes are not verified weights. Failures are unevenly distributed, especially for GLM, and conditional post-feedback groups change with the defense. Semantic recognition, question accuracy, consultation necessity, and deliverable quality need independent review. The four-reply core does not test objectively false advice, genuine partial authorization, or continuations from an identical pre-response state.

\bench{} connects intervention decisions to observable tool effects. Its results support evaluating three stages together: requesting the right help, interpreting its authority, and carrying it through to execution. The implemented SAIL method follows these same stages. Its new comparison tests whether scoped human permission preserves authorized execution beyond conservative blocking, while accounting for query and model costs.
