\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Across configured agents, safe human control required consultation coverage, correct authority interpretation, and preservation of authorized work. High completion concealed unsafe extensions, while high conditional success concealed missed consultation. SAIL v3 shifted failures toward reply interpretation and lost task obligations, supporting evaluation of the complete intervention loop.

These results connect the separate utility and safety checks used by AgentDojo with the feedback-use questions studied by HiL-Bench and HAS-Bench. They motivate preserving original authority when an extension is denied, rather than treating increased consultation as sufficient evidence of effective human control.

The conclusions concern 20 reused tasks with synthetic replies and simulated effects. Conditional feedback groups, uneven failures, and different serving conditions limit comparisons. Independent review of recognition, question meaning, consultation necessity, and deliverable quality is needed before interpreting these proxies as general agent capabilities. Held-out tasks and real feedback would test transfer; shared-prefix continuations would better isolate reply effects. SAIL v4 implements authority preservation and recovery, but its formal outcomes remain pending. Current evidence establishes the intervention failures that motivate this revision, without establishing its performance.
