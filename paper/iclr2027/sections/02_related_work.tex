\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Agent Safety Benchmarks}

Existing agent-safety benchmarks evaluate behavioral risks arising from planning,
tool use, and environment interaction. ToolEmu and R-Judge assess unsafe tool use
and risk awareness; AgentDojo and Agent-SafetyBench study indirect injection and
broader failures in executable environments
\citep{ruan2024toolemu,yuan2024rjudge,debenedetti2024agentdojo,zhang2024agentsafetybench}.
MT-AgentRisk studies multi-turn attacks, while SkillHarm and SkillSafetyBench
examine risks introduced through reusable skills
\citep{li2026unsafer,ning2026skillharm,jin2026skillsafetybench}.
HiL-Bench evaluates help-seeking under incomplete specifications; HAS-Bench and
HumanAgencyBench examine feedback, control, and agency support
\citep{trinh2026hilbench,wu2026hasbench,sturgeon2025humanagencybench}.
OverEager-Bench studies unauthorized scope expansion during benign tasks,
complementing evaluation under adversarial inputs \citep{qu2026overeager}.
Our focus is the interaction between pre-action intervention and post-response
control under indirect attacks. \bench{} varies approval validity and scope,
includes actions that remain prohibited after approval, and scores tool traces
and terminal environment effects. Table~\ref{tab:comparison} summarizes the
compared settings, including prior evaluation of human control.

\subsection{Agent Safety Defenses}

Agent-safety defenses range from text-level filtering to runtime enforcement.
Llama Guard, WildGuard, and ShieldGemma detect harmful prompts or responses but
do not directly constrain consequential tool effects
\citep{inan2023llamaguard,han2024wildguard,zeng2024shieldgemma}.
StruQ and SecAlign separate trusted instructions from untrusted data or improve
resistance to injected commands \citep{chen2025struq,chen2025secalign}.
Adaptive attacks demonstrate the need to evaluate defenses against attacks
tailored to their design \citep{zhan2025adaptive}.
GuardAgent, CaMeL, and ToolSafe monitor behavior or apply safeguards at tool-call
time \citep{xiang2025guardagent,debenedetti2025camel,toolsafe2026}.
Progent requires approval for privilege expansion, while SafeAgent combines
runtime control with recovery \citep{shi2025progent,liu2026safeagent}.
\sail{} focuses on interpreting human replies as scoped authorization for the
proposed effect while preserving original task authority. It binds approved
extensions to one-use execution permits and returns the agent to authorized
work after a denial. Semantic review remains fallible; our experiments compare
controller versions and ablations within \bench{}.
