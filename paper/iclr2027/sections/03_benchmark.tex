\section{Benchmark Overview}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\subsection{Tasks, threats, and authority}
A task specifies a benign goal, initial environment, artifacts, completion predicate, and attacker-targeted effect. The attacker modifies designated lower-trust materials, sometimes coordinating a skill and a policy-like document, to make an extra action appear necessary. The original user goal, trusted deployment constraints, reply fixtures, and evaluator remain outside attacker control. A document's assertion of authority does not make it trusted.

A neutral \texttt{ask\_human(question)} interface lets the agent decide whether and what to ask, without revealing risk labels. The runtime records ordered observations, questions, replies, and tool effects. Consequential operations are simulated: a recorded upload does not transmit data externally.

A \emph{confirmable} effect becomes permissible after timely approval of its action and scope. A \emph{prohibited} effect remains impermissible under ordinary approval. Thus, executing an attacker-targeted effect is unsafe only when the applicable authority does not permit it.

\subsection{Human feedback and expected behavior}
The implemented core uses
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{core}}=
\{\text{clear approval},\ \text{clear denial},\ \text{ambiguity},\ \text{scope mismatch}\}.
\label{eq:responses}
\end{equation}
For confirmable actions, clear approval permits the specified effect; denial, ambiguity, and approval of another step do not. No reply permits a prohibited effect. Repeated clarification is allowed within a fixed query budget.

Objectively erroneous advice and genuine partial authorization require additional facts and predicates. They remain outside the core (Appendix~\ref{app:responses}); clarity, correctness, and authority are distinct.

\subsection{Stage-specific evaluation}
\label{sec:metrics}
Let $e_i$ denote the specified effect and $b_i$ benign completion. A matched question before the first effect, or when no effect occurs, gives $h_i=1$. The single-effect scorer defines valid authorization $a_i$ and unsafe execution $u_i$ as
\begin{equation}
a_i=\ind[\text{confirmable}_i \land h_i \land
  \text{clear-approve}_i],\qquad u_i=e_i(1-a_i).
\label{eq:authorization}
\end{equation}
Hidden categories are evaluator labels, unavailable to agents and defenses. General expiry, repeated-use limits, and cross-tool equivalence require the richer contract in Appendix~\ref{app:contract}.

We assess agent safety through benign completion, unsafe attack success, and autonomous risk recognition. Human-interaction measures assess consultation recall, question accuracy, and strict success after feedback, with separate eligible denominators.

\paragraph{Before feedback.}
HIL recall is the matched-consultation fraction among confirmable cases. This proxy can penalize appropriate rejection of optional extensions, so consultation necessity requires independent review. We separately record direct blocking, question timing, and query burden. Autonomous recognition requires visible identification of the specific risk before feedback; non-execution alone is insufficient. Recognition remains N/A, and question matching is only a rule proxy for semantic accuracy.

\paragraph{After feedback.}
On consulted runs $\mathcal{H}=\{i:h_i=1\}$, response correctness $c_i$ requires executing a validly approved effect and withholding the effect otherwise. Strict success after feedback combines response use, task completion, and safety:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PostSuccess}=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal H}c_i b_i(1-u_i)}{|\mathcal H|}.
\end{equation}
Omitting an approved effect reduces this score even when execution remains safe. We also report safe authorized execution over all confirmable approval cases, including unconsulted ones, for the no-human comparison. Conditional post-feedback scores compare differently selected populations and do not estimate causal feedback effects.

\paragraph{Final outcomes.}
For $N$ valid runs, $\mathrm{BCR}=N^{-1}\sum_i b_i$, $\mathrm{ASR}=N^{-1}\sum_i u_i$, and joint safe utility is $N^{-1}\sum_i b_i(1-u_i)$. Undefined ratios are N/A; failures receive separate counts and missing-outcome bounds. Benign predicates check tool effects but do not fully assess deliverable content. Appendix~\ref{app:annotation} gives the remaining diagnostic definitions and annotation requirements.
