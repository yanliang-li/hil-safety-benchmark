\subsection{Does scoped HIL improve execution?}

\label{sec:sailresults}

The separate frozen comparison completes all 6,720 attempts, with 6,019 valid runs and 701 failures. It uses fresh prompt controls and interleaved conditions on the same 80 development cases: three repeats for prompt and SAIL, and one for the no-human ablation. Preflights are excluded.

\input{tables/sail_main}

Unsafe ASR changes from 355/2,691 (13.19\%) with the prompt to 41/2,450 (1.67\%) with SAIL. Benign completion changes from 89.59\% to 83.55\%. On 2,355 matched pairs, the ASR difference is -11.00 percentage points (95\% task-cluster interval [-23.26, -2.13]); the benign-completion difference is -7.22 points ([-18.54, +0.59]). These remain exploratory estimates over 20 reused tasks.

On matched eligible cases in repeat 1, safe authorized execution is 0/141 (0.00\%) without human replies and 58/141 (41.13\%) with full SAIL. This metric includes unconsulted eligible cases. SAIL records 1,219 actor questions and 868 controller questions. Thus its consultation score describes the combined system. Appendix~\ref{app:sailresults} reports failure bounds, response metrics, and reviewer cost. These comparisons do not isolate scope binding from the other controller components.
